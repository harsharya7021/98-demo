01
The Snapshot

TableSpoon is the Italian room — casual fine dining — inside the Yum Yum Cha group, a hospitality operator whose brand equity was built on an Asian flagship. The brief to 98 was to give TableSpoon a feed identity distinct from the parent brand: not 'Yum Yum Cha's other place', but a destination that earns its own creator-stack vocabulary. Italian is the most contested fine-casual category in Delhi. The cuisine buys no distinctiveness on its own — so the room has to do the work the menu can't.

98 mapped 17 creators across 64 story frames. Five marquee anchors above 260K each (Varun Verma 590K, Shreya Chadda 480K, Toshali Paliwal 360K, Harteerath Singh 297K, Saaniya Khanna 264K) — every one of them in a different audience class. In a category this crowded, five audience classes beat one loud one. The campaign positioned TableSpoon as an independent destination while quietly leveraging the operational depth of the Yum Yum Cha group behind it.

02
The Challenge

A contested category is its own positioning trap — the more familiar the cuisine, the less a menu can say about the room. TableSpoon had a second layer: it needed feed-identity independence from its parent (Yum Yum Cha) without breaking the operational continuity. The campaign had to give the venue a personality the menu couldn't supply, and quietly distinguish it from an umbrella brand whose equity is Asian.

  • Sub-brand identity trap. Sub-brands of recognised hospitality groups risk reading as 'the group's other place'. TableSpoon needed a creator-stack vocabulary distinct from Yum Yum Cha's Asian-flagship grammar.
  • A crowded category. Every Delhi neighbourhood has an Italian room. Cuisine can't be the hook when the hook is table stakes — creator-stack personality is what separates one from the next.
  • Five-class marquee. Each marquee creator from a different audience class — fashion, food, founder, lifestyle, digital creator. Five distinct audience reads in one venue.
  • Founder cameo. Harteerath Singh appears here as he did in Poetry and Farzi — a 98 pattern where founder-class creators do credibility work.
