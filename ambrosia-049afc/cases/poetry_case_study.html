The Snapshot

Poetry by Love & Cheesecake sits in the upscale all-day-café space — the room wins on architecture, not virality. The brand had run on word-of-mouth for years; the brief to 98 was to build a feed presence without breaking the room's quiet.

98 mapped one creator to each distinct audience class: Harteerath Singh Ahluwalia (297K verified · founder · philanthropist) for credibility outside the food bubble; Mallaica Chatterjee (269K verified · fashion · travel) for occasion bookings; Anchit & Surabhi (257K · couples · food) for date-night recall; Saaniya Khanna (264K verified · digital creator) for fashion-vertical lift. Four creators, four audience classes, near-zero overlap.

02
The Challenge

Poetry's trade is quiet — diners arrive for the architecture and the slow Sunday energy, not for a viral moment. A traditional 'food creator' campaign would have broken the room's tone. The brand needed feed presence + booking conversion + zero noise pollution. Four creators, four audience classes, one careful sound lock.

  • Quiet brands need careful voices. The wrong creator stack would have flattened Poetry into 'another nice café'. We needed voices whose own grammar already valued slowness.
  • Four audience classes, not four of the same. Four fashion creators would have multiplied reach, not addressable audience. One per class — founder, fashion-travel, couples, fashion-digital — opened up four non-overlapping pools.
  • Carla Bruni audio bed. Trending audio would have signalled 'campaign'. A French chanson — explicitly off-trend — read as room-mood. The audio became Poetry's unofficial wordmark.
  • Long-stay, not spike. The KPI was 30-day booking lift, not 7-day reach. Briefed creators for evergreen content, not algorithmic peak.
